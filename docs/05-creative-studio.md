## Overview

Creative Studio helps Sellers create, enhance, adapt, and prepare Discovery Media for PinkCurve Discovery. Discovery Media may also be created or provided directly by Sellers without being generated by Creative Studio.

Creative Studio is responsible exclusively for creating, enhancing, adapting, and preparing Discovery Media. It does not create, modify, or manage the authoritative attributes or operational state of an associated Discovery Service. It uses applicable Approved Source Knowledge and authorized creative assets to prepare presentation content and discovery-oriented metadata. Metadata representing authoritative service facts must remain consistent with the underlying Discovery Service.

For MVP, each Discovery Service entity may have **at most one associated Discovery Media**. Creative Studio can use multiple working assets or candidate drafts internally, but only one Discovery Media entity is associated with a given service entity. Creative Studio does not determine Discovery Classification, pricing, eligibility, or ranking.

Its purpose is broader than generating advertisements.

Creative Studio helps Sellers communicate why a Discovery service or its subject matter may be:

* Relevant

* Useful

* Interesting

* Timely

* Worth exploring

Creative Studio may work with:

* Seller-provided images

* Seller-provided videos

* Existing marketing materials

* Destination URLs

* AI-generated content

* AI-assisted modifications

* PinkCurve-created or assisted Discovery Media

The objective is to help create, enhance, adapt, or prepare Discovery Media that Buyers can understand quickly, particularly on mobile devices.

# Creative Philosophy

PinkCurve is a **visual-first discovery platform**.

Buyers should not have to read large amounts of promotional text before understanding a Discovery service or its subject matter.

Creative Studio therefore follows several principles.

### Understand Quickly

Discovery Media should communicate the main idea of the Discovery service or its subject matter within a short period of attention.

### Show Rather Than Explain

Images, video, demonstrations, scenes, and concise visual information should carry much of the message.

### Remain Grounded in Approved Source Knowledge

Discovery Media should accurately represent the Discovery service and its underlying subject matter and should not introduce unsupported claims.

### Preserve Authenticity

Seller-provided content may often be more authentic and informative than completely AI-generated content.

### Separate Truth From Presentation

Creative Studio may improve how a Discovery service and its subject matter are presented, but it should not invent their factual reality.

For factual characteristics of the Discovery service and its underlying subject matter, especially where visual appearance matters, the Seller or another authorized source should supply the underlying truth. PinkCurve may then organize, enhance, format, or present that truth effectively for Discovery.

This principle is especially important for Real-Time Discovery, where a

Seller may provide a current photo and a few factual details while

PinkCurve rapidly creates a useful visual presentation around them.

\> **Seller supplies truth → PinkCurve supplies presentation.**

### Support Buyer Discovery

Discovery Media should help Buyers decide whether the Discovery service or its subject matter deserves further exploration rather than pressure them into an action or transaction.

### Mobile First

Discovery Media must work well on small screens and in visual feed environments.

### Trustworthy

Discovery Media must not intentionally mislead Buyers or disguise important limitations.

# Creative Studio Is Not an Ad Generator

Traditional advertising systems primarily ask:

\> How can we persuade someone to click or buy?

PinkCurve asks a different question:

\> How can we help someone quickly understand whether this discovery may matter to them?

This distinction affects Creative Studio design.

Creative Studio may help create or prepare advertising-style Discovery Media, but its primary goal is effective Discovery communication.

Effective Discovery Media helps the Buyer quickly answer questions such as:

* What is this?

* Why might I care?

* Who is it for?

* What is different about it?

* Is it relevant to me?

* Should I explore it further?

The destination page remains the place where the buyer can investigate details and, where applicable, complete a transaction.

2. Briefs

4. Storyboards

10. Video segments

Working assets, temporary candidates, and reusable production components are not separately eligible Discovery Media.

## Rapid Real-Time Discovery Media

Real-Time Discovery may require a much simpler creative path than longer-lived Discovery services or campaigns.

A Seller may provide only:

* A current photo or other authorized factual media

* A short description

* Price or discount where applicable

* Current availability or quantity where applicable

* Location

* Expiration or availability period

Creative Studio may use this information to rapidly create, enhance, or prepare simple Discovery Media such as a poster, card, image, or other feed-oriented presentation.

If no factual photo is available, PinkCurve may create a

typography-based or graphic presentation, but it should not generate an

image that falsely appears to depict the actual subject of the Real-Time Discovery.

Real-Time Discovery Media should prioritize:

* Speed

* Factual accuracy

* Clarity

* Local relevance where applicable

* Freshness

* Low generation cost

Rich scripts, storyboards, video generation, and multiple creative variants should not be required when they would add unnecessary delay or cost to short-lived Real-Time Discovery.

\> **The richness of Discovery Media should be proportional to the lifetime and purpose of the Discovery service.**

# Creative Workflow

Creative Studio supports a lightweight path for existing Seller media and a richer optional path for media needing production. In both cases, the authoritative Discovery Service is managed separately and supplies the applicable facts and operational state.

```text
Seller-provided media / Approved Source Knowledge / Authorized assets
                              ↓
                 Create / Select / Adapt
                              ↓
                    Seller reviews draft
                              ↓
               Discovery Media — Draft
                              ↓
                   Submit → Review
                              ↓
                  PinkCurve approval
                              ↓
               Discovery Media — Approved
                              ↓
    Discovery Engine checks service + media eligibility
                              ↓
                       Buyer Discovery
```

Sellers create, edit, review, and submit Discovery Media through the **Seller web interface**, not the mobile app. Sellers may view their media and use Buyer-style discovery on mobile. Buyers may use the web or mobile app for Buyer functionality.

A Seller may use an existing suitable image or video without AI generation. Real-Time Discovery should favor fast, low-cost posters or feed cards using current authorized facts, but it must still meet required Seller review, PinkCurve approval, and trust checks. Approval time and service start/expiry conditions remain governed by the underlying Real-Time Discovery service.

# Working Candidates — Optional Creative Selection

Creative Studio may identify or produce several **Working Candidates** to help a Seller select an effective creative approach. A candidate may be a concept, headline, poster, visual arrangement, storyboard, or assembled media draft; candidates are production alternatives, **not separate approved Discovery Media entities**.

For example, a Seller may compare a feature-focused candidate, a benefit-focused candidate, and a promotion-focused candidate. The Seller may choose one, request adjustments, or provide their own suitable media. Candidate creation is optional: a complete, effective Seller asset or a short-lived Real-Time opportunity should not incur unnecessary AI-generation cost or delay.

```mermaid
flowchart LR
    IN[Approved Source Knowledge and Authorized Materials] --> DEC{Alternatives useful?}
    DEC -->|No| ONE[Use or Adapt Suitable Asset]
    DEC -->|Yes| CAND[Optional Working Candidates]
    CAND --> SEL[Seller Selects One]
    SEL --> REF[Refine Selected Candidate]
    ONE --> DRAFT[One Discovery Media Draft]
    REF --> DRAFT
    DRAFT --> SEC[Trust and Security Validation]
    SEC --> REV[Seller Review and PinkCurve Approval]
```

Selection criteria should prioritize factual accuracy, Buyer comprehension, relevance, accessibility, mobile presentation, trust, and incremental cost. Candidate counts and generation effort should be bounded by the expected value and lifetime of the Discovery Service. Unselected candidates remain working materials and are not delivered to Buyers.

# 5. Review and Approval

New or materially modified Discovery Media should not automatically become eligible for use in Discovery.

The normal workflow should support Seller review before applicable PinkCurve review and approval.

```text
Discovery Media — Draft
       ↓ Seller submits
Discovery Media — Review
       ↓ PinkCurve approves
Discovery Media — Approved
       ↓ Service and media eligibility checks
Available to Discovery Engine
```

Discovery Media approval does not by itself authorize the associated Discovery service for active Discovery. The associated Discovery service must independently satisfy its applicable approval, trust, validity, freshness, and Discovery-readiness requirements.

Review may check:

* Discovery service and subject-matter accuracy

* Brand alignment

* Visual quality

* Unsupported claims

* Required disclosures

* Destination accuracy

* Trust and policy compliance

PinkCurve may perform automated or human review of Discovery Media according to applicable risk and approval policies.

Discovery Media review should be risk-based.

New Sellers, first submissions of a Discovery service, materially changed Discovery Media, sensitive claims, suspicious content, or other higher-risk cases may require human review in addition to automated checks.

For trusted Sellers with a strong history, PinkCurve may eventually

allow automated approval of defined low-risk Discovery Media changes when

applicable policy permits, while retaining monitoring and audit

evidence.

For rapid Real-Time Discovery Media, automated checks may evaluate factors such as factual consistency, required information, image and text consistency, destination integrity, prohibited content, and other risk signals before determining whether human review is required.

High AI confidence should not bypass human review where PinkCurve

policy requires human approval.

# Mandatory Trust & Security Validation

**All Discovery Media and associated metadata must pass appropriate Trust & Security Engine validation before PinkCurve approval and Buyer-facing publication, regardless of origin.** Seller-uploaded assets, authorized destination-sourced materials, third-party outputs, and content generated inside Creative Studio are subject to the same publication gate. Internally generated media is not trusted merely because PinkCurve produced it.

## Asset Ingestion and Technical Safety

Untrusted inputs should be quarantined until they pass appropriate security checks. Controls may include:

- Malware and malicious-content scanning for uploaded and retrieved assets.
- Actual file-type, size, encoding, structural, and media-decoder validation, rather than relying on filename extensions.
- Restricted, isolated processing of potentially dangerous files and media.
- Removal or safe handling of unnecessary active content, unsafe embedded links, and sensitive metadata.
- Destination URL and redirect integrity checks, including protections against unauthorized network access.
- Appropriate scanning of the **final rendered media** as well as its inputs, particularly after transformations or new asset generation.

A scan result is not proof that a file is risk-free. Suspicious, unsupported, or unvalidated assets must be blocked or held for review; they must not be published.

## Content, Privacy, and Metadata Safety

Validation should examine misleading claims, prohibited content, intellectual-property or usage-right concerns, confidential or personal information leakage, unauthorized brand use, embedded text, captions, voice-over, destination links, and associated metadata. Security checks complement, but do not replace, factual grounding and Seller confirmation.

The Trust & Security Engine may return **Pass**, **Needs Review**, or **Block**, with reason codes, evidence references, timestamps, and the evaluated asset/media identifier. A Pass does not itself grant PinkCurve approval.

## Approval and Revalidation Boundary

```mermaid
flowchart TD
    A[Seller / Authorized Destination / Creative Studio] --> B[Quarantine and Input Validation]
    B --> C[Creative Preparation and Final Rendering]
    C --> D[Trust & Security Engine: Final Media + Metadata]
    D -->|Block| E[Reject or Correct in Draft]
    D -->|Needs Review| F[Required Human or Specialist Review]
    D -->|Pass| G[Seller Review and Submission]
    F -->|Resolved| G
    F -->|Unresolved| E
    G --> H[PinkCurve Approval Checklist]
    H -->|Approved| I[Approved Discovery Media]
    H -->|Changes required| E
    I --> J[Service and Media Eligibility Checks]
    J --> K[Discovery Engine]
```

A material change to approved media or its governed metadata returns the **same Discovery Media entity** to Draft and requires resubmission, revalidation, and reapproval. Newly identified threats may cause immediate suppression even if the media was previously approved. Risk-based automation can accelerate low-risk Real-Time workflows, but may not bypass mandatory checks or required human approval.

# Discovery Media Review and Approval Checklist

The checklist is a **single, shared, configurable framework** used first for Seller self-review and then for PinkCurve validation and approval. It applies to the final creative and its associated structured metadata, not merely to the visible image or video. The Seller web interface should show missing fields, actionable errors, and submission readiness without imposing unnecessary complexity.

| Area | Seller self-review | PinkCurve validation |
|---|---|---|
| Media content and technical quality | Confirm correct, legible, mobile-appropriate image/video | Verify format, rendering, duration, accessibility and technical constraints |
| Factual accuracy and claims | Confirm representation and claims against approved information | Compare material claims with applicable Approved Source Knowledge |
| Metadata completeness | Fill required fields for the service and classification | Validate required schema, types, controlled values, and relationships |
| Metadata accuracy and provenance | Confirm inferred or extracted attributes | Check source, confidence, authorization, and conflicts with authoritative service facts |
| Discovery Classification | Confirm the service's assigned classification is represented accurately | Validate consistency; Creative Studio cannot change classification |
| Validity and freshness | Confirm finite media start/end and current claims | Check dates, time zone, expiration, and authoritative service eligibility separately |
| Destination and rights | Confirm destination and rights to creative materials | Check URL integrity, redirects, permissions, and policy |
| Trust, security, privacy | Resolve flagged assets and sensitive information | Require Trust & Security Engine result and any mandated review |
| Buyer experience | Confirm quick comprehension and truthful presentation | Check clarity, mobile suitability, accessibility, and misleading emphasis |
| Seller representation | Confirm brand, identity, and permissions | Apply applicable impersonation, brand, and policy checks |

**Classification-aware extensions** should cover Real-Time Discovery (availability, quantity/capacity, location, expiry and approval timing), Brand Recognition (authorized brand representation), Offering Discovery (offering attributes and claims), and Community/Public services (appropriate source, locality, and applicable disclosures). Promotional facets such as Discount or Trending do not become independent Discovery Classifications.

## Review Outcomes and Responsibilities

- **Seller:** reviews and confirms creative content, key attributes, and inferred facts; submits through the Seller web interface.
- **Creative Studio:** prepares media and metadata, provides checklist feedback, and corrects production issues; does not approve its own output.
- **Trust & Security Engine:** performs mandatory technical/content/security validation and records findings.
- **PinkCurve approval function:** applies policy and the checklist, including human review where required, then approves or returns the same media entity to Draft.
- **Discovery Engine:** independently checks Approved status, finite media validity, and current Discovery Service eligibility before delivery.

The checklist is an approval control, **not** a replacement for the authoritative service-specific approval and operational-readiness checks. A media approval does not imply service readiness. The detailed policy should be reconciled with **PD-007** in Chapter 19, including minimum requirements for MVP.

# Creative Studio Purpose and Presentation

Creative Studio does not define PinkCurve's Discovery Service taxonomy, Discovery Classification, or billing rules. A Discovery service determines what is being submitted for Discovery, while Creative Studio determines how that service may be visually communicated.

Creative Studio should therefore distinguish among the underlying Discovery Service, its single Discovery Classification, the purpose or presentation of Discovery Media, and the media format. Discount, Nearby, Limited, New, and Trending are not separate billable Discovery Classifications merely because they influence presentation; the classification is defined by the underlying service and governs applicable pricing rules.

# Duration

Short-form video may commonly use durations such as:

* 10–15 seconds for rapid discovery

* 20–30 seconds for fuller explanation

* Up to **2 minutes** for general Discovery Media when the subject requires fuller explanation or demonstration

General Discovery Media may run for up to **2 minutes** when justified by Buyer understanding. This is an upper bound, not a target; short media should remain the default when sufficient. Brand Recognition Discovery may run up to **5 minutes** under its separately defined policy. Media duration is distinct from the finite start and end date/time that determine media validity.

The appropriate duration depends on:

* Complexity of the Discovery service or its subject matter

* Buyer context

* Creative format

* Discovery placement

* Performance evidence

# Discovery Media Variants — Future Capability

**MVP boundary:** Each Discovery Service entity has at most one associated Discovery Media entity. Multiple simultaneously eligible media variants per service entity, automated creative selection, and A/B testing are **not part of MVP**.

Creative Studio may generate several *working candidates* (e.g., feature-focused, benefit-focused, story-oriented, or promotion-oriented concepts) for Seller review, but the Seller selects a single Discovery Media for submission. Unselected candidate assets are not independent Discovery Media eligible for Discovery.

A later release may support multiple approved variants per Discovery Service entity, subject to a new architecture and explicit rules for identity, approval, selection, measurement, and billing. That future capability must not be inferred from the MVP data model.

Creative evaluation should consider Buyer relevance, exploration, positive and negative feedback, Discovery quality, trust, and Seller value—not click-through rate alone.

---

# Creative Campaigns — Future Organizational Capability

A Creative Campaign is an optional way to organize creative work, briefs, and source assets. It is **not required for MVP**, is not a Discovery Service or Discovery Classification, and does not create additional Discovery Media slots for a service entity. Campaign scheduling does not override the authoritative validity or eligibility of the underlying Discovery Service or the finite validity period of its Discovery Media.

If a later release introduces a persisted Creative Campaign entity, its proposed fields may include `campaign_id`, `seller_id`, `discovery_service_id`, `title`, `objective`, `target_context`, and organizational status. Ownership flows through `seller_id`; `organization_id` is derived through the Seller relationship. Exact fields, lifecycle, and relationships must be decided in Data Architecture before implementation.

For MVP, creative briefs and working assets can be organized within the Seller's creative workspace without a separate campaign entity. Previously implemented campaign containers may remain as internal tooling but must not dictate the platform's authoritative Discovery Media model.

# Discovery Media Lifecycle

Discovery Media follows the simple approval lifecycle established in Product Architecture:

```mermaid
stateDiagram-v2
    [*] --> Draft
    Draft --> Review: Seller submits
    Review --> Approved: PinkCurve approves
    Review --> Draft: Changes required
    Approved --> Draft: Seller modifies approved media
    Draft --> Review: Seller resubmits
```

**Draft** is editable and not eligible for Discovery. **Review** is submitted and awaiting applicable PinkCurve checks or approval. **Approved** means the Discovery Media passed its review, but does not by itself mean the associated Discovery Service is eligible for active Discovery.

Any modification to an Approved Discovery Media record returns that same entity to **Draft** and requires resubmission and reapproval before it can be used again. The `discovery_media_id` remains unchanged. MVP does not maintain separately approved media versions or version history solely for audit or rollback.

Operational conditions such as active, paused, expired, unavailable, or suppressed are **Discovery eligibility / serving states**, not additional Discovery Media approval states. Every Discovery Media has a finite validity start and end time; no open-ended validity. The Discovery Engine must verify both media approval and validity and the associated Discovery Service's current eligibility before serving the media.

Audit events, provenance, Discovery events, and billing records may be retained under their own stable identifiers and references without requiring separate historical versions of Discovery Media. A trust or safety issue may suppress use of Approved media pending appropriate action.

# Grounding and Accuracy

Discovery Media must remain grounded in applicable Approved Source Knowledge.

AI should not invent:

* Product features

* Discounts

* Testimonials

* Certifications

* Performance claims

* Availability

* Prices

* Medical or safety claims

* Seller guarantees

without appropriate source information and verification.

PinkCurve should maintain traceability between important claims or representations in Discovery Media and their approved knowledge sources where practical.

Visual representations in Discovery Media should follow the same grounding requirements as textual claims.

When the actual appearance of the subject being represented is material to Buyer understanding, Creative Studio should distinguish Seller-provided or otherwise authorized factual imagery from AI-generated presentation imagery.

AI-generated imagery should not be presented in a way that falsely implies that it is factual imagery of the actual product, food, place, service result, event, community resource, public information, or other subject being represented in Discovery Media.

The same grounding and provenance requirements should apply regardless of whether Discovery Media is Seller-provided, Creative Studio-assisted, or AI-generated.

PinkCurve should preserve sufficient Discovery Media provenance to support this distinction and downstream trust, review, and auditing.

# Creative Quality

Creative quality is broader than visual polish.

Potential dimensions include:

### Accuracy

Does the Discovery Media accurately represent applicable facts, claims, conditions, and Approved Source Knowledge?

### Freshness

For time-sensitive Discovery services, does the Discovery Media still represent current Approved Source Knowledge, including applicable availability, price, quantity or capacity, validity period, and expiration?

### Clarity

Can the buyer quickly understand what is being shown?

### Visual Quality

Is the content suitable for PinkCurve's visual experience?

### Relevance

Does the Discovery Media communicate information that matters in its Discovery context?

### Authenticity

Does the Discovery Media accurately reflect the Seller, the applicable Discovery service and its subject matter, and the brand where relevant?

### Trust

Does it avoid misleading or manipulative representation?

### Mobile Suitability

Can it be understood on a small screen?

### Accessibility

Where appropriate, content should support capabilities such as captions, readable text, and other accessibility requirements.

# Discovery Media Metadata — Structure, Sources, and Governance

Discovery Media includes **presentation content** and a governed set of **discovery-oriented metadata**. Metadata makes media understandable, searchable, filterable, and navigable at scale. Creative Studio prepares and proposes metadata; the Discovery Media management and approval workflow governs accepted values. AMN and the Discovery Engine consume eligible metadata but do not rely on Creative Studio to implement navigation or ranking.

## How Metadata Is Included

Each Discovery Media entity has its stable `discovery_media_id` and association with the applicable Discovery Service entity. Its metadata is stored as structured fields or governed attribute/value records **associated with that same media entity**, not burned exclusively into pixels or video frames. Selected values may also be displayed as text, captions, badges, or overlays, but the structured metadata remains independently accessible for AMN, search, validation, and analytics. Physical schema choices belong in Data Architecture.

For MVP, a Discovery Service entity has at most one Discovery Media entity. Multiple metadata attributes on that entity do **not** constitute multiple media variants. A metadata change that modifies an Approved Discovery Media record follows the established **Draft → Review → Approved** lifecycle, retains the same media ID, and requires reapproval before the modified media is used. Changes to live authoritative operational facts are managed by the Discovery Service and checked at discovery time; they do not automatically become unapproved edits to the media entity.

## Metadata Categories and Examples

| Category | Illustrative values | Authority and treatment |
|---|---|---|
| Identity and linkage | `discovery_media_id`, `discovery_service_id`, associated `seller_id` | Platform-managed identifiers and relationships; Seller relationship is authoritative |
| Media technical properties | Image/video type, MIME/format, width, height, aspect ratio, resolution, video length, language, captions | Derived from the actual media or validated input |
| Presentation descriptors | Title, short annotation, visible subject, scene, style, demonstration type, accessibility description | Seller-confirmed or appropriately reviewed AI-assisted descriptions |
| Stable subject taxonomy | Fashion, footwear, electronics, restaurant, community event; governed subtypes | Governed taxonomy; avoid inventing incompatible free-form categories |
| Subject-specific attributes | Color, size, material, waterproof, terrain, intended use, country of origin | Source-backed service/Offering facts when factual; references or derived representations, not conflicting independent facts |
| Geography | Country, state, city, postal code, address when available, latitude/longitude | Derived from authorized service/location facts; apply privacy and precision controls |
| Seller-declared merchandising | Discount, special offer, free delivery, limited-time promotion | Grounded in the service's current approved promotion and validity conditions |
| Computed discovery signals | New, Trending, popularity or freshness signals | Determined by PinkCurve systems; not freely asserted by Creative Studio or Seller |
| Validity and timing | Media valid-from and valid-until timestamps; media duration | Media validity always has finite start and end; service availability is separately authoritative |
| Provenance and governance | Source type/reference, extraction method, confidence if inferred, verification state, last checked time | Traceability for review, correction, and safe use |

This is an **extensible metadata framework**, not a requirement that every service populate every field. Applicable attributes vary by Discovery Service and subject type. Required fields and controlled vocabularies should be governed centrally and defined in service-specific schemas where appropriate.

## Metadata Creation and Validation Workflow

1. **Collect:** Read authorized Seller-provided media, Seller-entered descriptions, and applicable Approved Source Knowledge and service attributes.
2. **Extract:** Obtain objective media properties (for example, image dimensions, video duration, file format, and supported language/caption details).
3. **Suggest:** Use AI, where useful, to identify visible subjects and propose relevant descriptive tags or attribute values. Mark uncertain inferences rather than treating them as verified facts.
4. **Reconcile:** Compare proposed factual attributes with authoritative Discovery Service information; reject or flag unsupported or conflicting claims. Dynamic conditions such as quantity, price, and availability must be read from the service at the time they matter.
5. **Normalize:** Map accepted values to governed names, types, units, controlled vocabularies, and appropriate geographic granularity; avoid duplicate synonyms and uncontrolled tag proliferation.
6. **Seller review:** Present the metadata alongside a media preview in the Seller **web interface**, allowing correction or confirmation of Seller-controllable values.
7. **PinkCurve review and approval:** Validate content, metadata, provenance, and policy requirements together before the media becomes Approved.
8. **Publish for discovery:** Make approved structured metadata available to Discovery Engine and AMN. Reconcile current service eligibility and computed signals at discovery time.

## Metadata Ownership and Change Rules

Creative Studio may **propose or prepare** media metadata, but it cannot override authoritative service data. Media-native facts such as dimensions may be machine-extracted; Seller assertions need appropriate grounding; AI-inferred attributes require provenance and review; PinkCurve-derived signals remain under their originating system's control. Do not duplicate operational state as independently editable media metadata. Instead, resolve current authoritative values or references when rendering and matching media.

A Discovery Media record may have many metadata values, including combinations that AMN can expose on small screens. The AMN interface chooses which values to surface, compress, or combine based on discovery context; the metadata itself should remain structured, extensible, and traceable.

# Creative Studio and Adaptive Metadata Navigation

Creative Studio prepares Discovery Media presentation content and may suggest **discovery-oriented metadata** derived from Seller input, authorized media, AI extraction/inference, and approved source knowledge. Metadata must carry appropriate provenance and governance. Service-specific attributes and operational state remain authoritative in the underlying Discovery Service; media metadata must reference or derive from those facts rather than create conflicting copies.

Adaptive Metadata Navigation (AMN) makes relevant metadata visible and usable for discovery. Buyers may select **one or multiple metadata values**, combine them, and add, remove, or change selections without following a fixed category hierarchy. The system must organize and compress potentially large metadata sets for mobile presentation and rapidly surface matching Discovery Media even when those items would otherwise appear far down a feed.

For example, a Buyer viewing trail-running shoes may combine `Waterproof`, `Lightweight`, and `Terrain: Trail`, then remove `Lightweight` to broaden the results. Creative Studio does not implement AMN ranking or navigation logic; it supplies governed media metadata to the platform's Discovery Engine and AMN capabilities.

Metadata types must distinguish stable taxonomy and service facts from Seller-declared promotions and dynamic computed signals such as New or Trending. Creative Studio must not independently assert PinkCurve-computed Trending status.

Approved Discovery Media may remain stored and reusable even when its associated Discovery service is temporarily or permanently ineligible for active Discovery.

The Discovery Engine should present Approved Discovery Media only when the associated Discovery service satisfies applicable Discovery-readiness conditions, including current validity, availability, freshness, trust, and other applicable requirements.

# AI Video Generation

Creative Studio may use AI video generation as one capability for creating, enhancing, or adapting video-based Discovery Media. AI-generated video is not required for Discovery Media, and Discovery Media may instead be provided directly by Sellers or created through other authorized methods.

Video-generation technology continues to evolve in:

* Quality

* Reliability

* Cost

* Latency

* Control

* Brand consistency

PinkCurve can therefore use a hybrid strategy:

```text

Seller Information / Approved Source Knowledge / Creative Assets

                         ↓

                  Creative Studio

                         ↓

             AI-Assisted Video Creation

                         ↓

                  Discovery Media

                         ↓

                  Seller Review

                         ↓

             PinkCurve Review / Approval

                         ↓

            Approved Discovery Media

                         ↓

                    Discovery

```

AI-generated video should follow the same Discovery Media grounding, provenance, Seller-control, review, approval, and lifecycle requirements as Discovery Media created through other methods.

As video-generation quality improves, PinkCurve can increase its use without changing the core architecture.

Video should not become the default Discovery Media format merely because it

can be generated.

Long-lived Discovery services may justify richer internal creative work involving strategy, scripts, storyboards, and multiple working assets; multiple simultaneously active Discovery Media variants are post-MVP.

Short-lived Discovery services, especially Real-Time Discovery, should generally favor simpler, faster, and lower-cost Discovery Media formats when those formats communicate the Discovery service and its subject matter effectively.

\> **The richness of Discovery Media should be proportional to the lifetime and purpose of the Discovery service.**

# Evaluation Hypotheses

Creative Studio should validate several hypotheses rather than assume them.

### Hypothesis 1

AI-assisted Creative Studio capabilities can reduce the cost and effort required for Sellers to create, enhance, adapt, or prepare Discovery Media for PinkCurve.

### Hypothesis 2

Effective Discovery Media helps Buyers understand the applicable Discovery service and its subject matter more quickly.

### Hypothesis 3

In a post-MVP release, multiple Discovery Media variants may improve Discovery when optimization considers Buyer usefulness rather than clicks alone; MVP supports one media entity per service entity.

### Hypothesis 4

Seller-provided Discovery Media combined with AI assistance may often outperform fully AI-generated Discovery Media.

### Hypothesis 5

Discovery Media performance learning can improve future Discovery and Seller outcomes.

These hypotheses should be tested through actual platform data.

---

### Hypothesis 6

A rapid Discovery Media workflow using Seller-provided factual information, Seller-provided Discovery Media or creative assets, and simple PinkCurve-assisted presentation can produce sufficiently clear, trustworthy, and useful Discovery Media for time-sensitive Discovery without requiring the full creative-production workflow.

# Rapid Real-Time Discovery as a Strategic Differentiator

Rapid Real-Time Discovery is a first-class, optimized path through the Creative Pipeline, not a separate incompatible media architecture. Its value is the integrated experience: **Seller opportunity → low-friction creative preparation → mandatory security and approval → timely Buyer Discovery → measurable Seller value**. AI poster generation alone is not a defensible advantage.

A typical Seller should be able to supply a current photo, concise description, and applicable price or discount, location, availability, and expiration. Creative Studio should reuse those assets, prepare a readable poster or feed card, validate required metadata, and route it through Seller confirmation and PinkCurve checks with minimal avoidable delay and expense.

The service's approval-dependent start, minimum duration, extension, and expiration rules remain authoritative outside Creative Studio. Creative Studio must not promise delivery before approval or display expired opportunities as current.

**MVP validation metrics** should include Seller time to create/submit media, PinkCurve approval latency, total time to eligible Discovery, cost per approved media item, Buyer engagement and negative feedback, and repeat Seller use. Competitive advantage and network effects are hypotheses to validate, not guaranteed outcomes.

# Cost-Efficient, Revenue-Aligned Creative Operations

PinkCurve should minimize fixed operating costs and align variable expenses with actual usage and revenue-generating activities while maintaining healthy contribution margins. Creative Studio should use suitable existing Seller materials first, perform only necessary transformations, and select economical AI models and infrastructure when generation is justified.

- Avoid mandatory AI generation, candidate proliferation, or expensive video production when simpler media is effective.
- Prefer lightweight templates and bounded compute for short-lived Real-Time opportunities.
- Use managed or usage-based infrastructure where economically appropriate, while evaluating minimum commitments and per-use fees.
- Preserve mandatory security and approval checks; cost optimization must not bypass them.
- Track per-item generation, storage, validation, approval, and delivery-related costs against applicable Seller revenue and Buyer/Seller outcomes.

A low fixed-cost architecture is not sufficient on its own: each revenue-generating workflow should aim for a sustainable contribution margin after its incremental costs, including payment processing and trust controls.

# Design Principles

### Visual Discovery Comes First

Discovery Media should communicate visually whenever visual presentation helps Buyers understand the Discovery service or its subject matter quickly and naturally.

Visual-first does not mean video-first or production-heavy. Images, posters, cards, short video, Seller-provided media, and other appropriate formats may all support effective visual Discovery.

Creative exists to make Discovery easier, not to add unnecessary production complexity.

### Use Existing Discovery Media When It Works

PinkCurve should use suitable Seller-provided or existing authorized Discovery Media when it effectively supports Discovery. Creative Studio should not regenerate, replace, or materially modify Discovery Media merely because AI generation or other creative-production capabilities are available.

### Match Creative Effort to Discovery Service Purpose

Creative effort should reflect the purpose, lifetime, complexity, and presentation needs of the Discovery service.

Long-lived Discovery services may justify richer Discovery Media and more extensive creative production, while short-lived Discovery services, especially Real-Time Discovery, should generally favor simpler and faster approaches when those approaches communicate effectively.

PinkCurve should not add creative complexity that increases cost or

latency without materially improving Buyer understanding.

\> **The richness of Discovery Media should be proportional to the lifetime and purpose of the Discovery service.**

### Ground Creative in Knowledge

Claims and representations in Discovery Media should be grounded in applicable Approved Source Knowledge.

Creative Studio may improve presentation, but it should not invent or materially alter the factual reality of the Discovery service or its subject matter.

### Seller Control Matters

Sellers should retain appropriate control over how they, their Discovery services, their subject matter, and their brands are represented through Discovery Media.

When Creative Studio creates or materially modifies Discovery Media on behalf of a Seller, the Seller should have an appropriate opportunity to review the representation before applicable PinkCurve review and approval.

Seller review does not replace any PinkCurve review or approval required by applicable trust, safety, or Discovery policies.

### AI Assists Rather Than Defines Creative Studio

Creative Studio must continue to work regardless of which AI generation technologies succeed. AI is a capability used to assist in creating, enhancing, adapting, analyzing, or preparing Discovery Media; it does not define Creative Studio or the Discovery Media architecture.

### Learn From Performance

Discovery Media and Creative Studio practices should improve from real Discovery evidence, including Buyer interactions, usefulness signals, negative feedback, and other applicable performance evidence.

### Do Not Optimize for Clicks Alone

Discovery Media performance should not be optimized for clicks alone. Evaluation should consider Buyer usefulness, relevance, trust, negative feedback, and Seller value.

### Trust Is Required

Discovery Media must accurately and honestly represent the applicable Discovery service and its subject matter. Misleading, unsupported, or deceptive Discovery Media damages trust in the entire PinkCurve Discovery ecosystem.
