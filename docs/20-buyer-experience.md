## Overview

PinkCurve is a Discovery Platform designed to help Buyers discover Offerings that matter.

**Buyer Experience is the PinkCurve product responsible for the primary interaction between Buyers and the PinkCurve platform.**

It provides the buyer-facing experience through which Buyers:

* see and explore Offerings;
* browse or direct discovery;
* interact with Adaptive Metadata Navigation;
* communicate discovery intent;
* provide positive or negative feedback;
* save or share Offerings where supported;
* view appropriate trust and discovery signals;
* explore Seller destinations;
* control and redirect their discovery journey.

Buyer Experience does not independently perform all of the intelligence required to satisfy these interactions.

Instead, it works with other PinkCurve products and foundational capabilities, including:

* AI Discovery;
* Buyer Intelligence;
* Adaptive Metadata Navigation;
* Offering Knowledge;
* Trust;
* Discovery Analytics;
* Learning Engine;
* Data Architecture;
* other authorized PinkCurve capabilities.

Conceptually:

```text
                         Buyer
                           │
                           ▼
                  Buyer Experience
                           │
          ┌────────────────┼────────────────┐
          ▼                ▼                ▼
         AMN        Buyer Intelligence   AI Discovery
          │                │                │
          └────────────────┼────────────────┘
                           │
                 Other PinkCurve Products
                 and Shared Capabilities
                           │
                           ▼
                  Buyer Experience
                           │
                           ▼
                         Buyer
```

Buyer Experience presents the appropriate results of these products through a simple, visual, understandable interface while capturing Buyer interactions that allow PinkCurve to respond, learn, measure, and improve.

The Buyer Experience is built around one central objective:

> **Meaningful Discovery.**

PinkCurve should not measure the quality of the Buyer Experience by how many Offerings it can display, how long a Buyer remains on the platform, or how much content the Buyer consumes.

A Buyer who quickly discovers one worthwhile Offering may represent a more successful experience than a Buyer who scrolls through fifty Offerings without finding anything useful.

PinkCurve therefore seeks to reduce discovery burden rather than increase it.

The underlying technology may become increasingly sophisticated.

The Buyer Experience should remain simple.

> **Sophisticated underneath. Simple on the surface.**

---

## What PinkCurve Is

A simple description should remain understandable throughout the Buyer Experience:

> **What is PinkCurve? — A Discovery Platform.**

> **What is PinkCurve trying to create? — Meaningful Discovery.**

PinkCurve is not primarily:

- A marketplace
- A search engine
- An advertising feed
- A social media platform
- A transaction platform
- A catalog of listings

PinkCurve helps Buyers discover worthwhile products, services, promotions, events, community resources, public information, and other Offerings.

Transactions remain with Sellers unless PinkCurve's business model explicitly changes in the future.

---

## Buyer Experience North Star

Every significant Buyer Experience decision should return to one question:

> **Does this help the Buyer discover something worthwhile more quickly, easily, and confidently?**

A second question should follow:

> **Does PinkCurve reduce the Buyer's discovery burden—or add to it?**

If additional metadata, AI capabilities, controls, advertisements, navigation elements, or other features increase complexity without materially improving Meaningful Discovery, they should not be allowed to complicate the Buyer Experience.

---

# Visual First

PinkCurve is designed primarily for mobile and small-screen experiences while also providing an effective desktop experience.

Buyers should not need to read long Offering descriptions, advertisements, or lists of Offerings to discover something useful.

Offerings should be presented primarily through:

- Graphics
- Images
- Short videos
- Visual stories
- Other appropriate visual formats

The intended experience remains simple:

> **Open PinkCurve → See → Swipe → Discover → Explore**

A Buyer should be able to understand PinkCurve within seconds.

The Buyer should not need to understand PinkCurve's AI, algorithms, metadata systems, Learning Engine, Seller advertising model, or other underlying technologies.

PinkCurve's technology may be complicated.

Using PinkCurve should not be.

---

# Two Modes of Meaningful Discovery

PinkCurve should support two fundamental Buyer discovery behaviors.

## Browsing Discovery

Sometimes Buyers do not know exactly what they want.

They may simply want to see:

- What's new
- What's interesting
- What's nearby
- What's on sale
- What's happening today
- What useful services exist
- What other people are discovering
- What may be relevant to their interests
- What they did not know existed

This resembles walking through a good shopping district, mall, community event, or marketplace simply to see what is available.

The experience should be pleasing, relaxing, visually interesting, and easy to navigate.

The Buyer should not feel pressured to purchase something.

PinkCurve creates an opportunity for discovery.

The Buyer decides whether to continue.

---

## Directed Discovery

At other times, the Buyer has a clearer objective.

The Buyer may want to discover:

- A particular type of product
- A service
- A nearby business
- A discount
- An event
- A community service
- A public resource
- A seasonal Offering
- Something within a price range
- Something meeting particular characteristics

PinkCurve should help the Buyer progressively direct discovery without requiring complicated queries or large filter forms.

The Buyer may begin broadly and progressively narrow the discovery journey.

---

## The Two Modes Can Blend

Browsing and Directed Discovery should not be separate systems.

A Buyer may begin by browsing, discover something interesting, and then become more specific.

Likewise, a Buyer searching for something specific may discover related Offerings they did not initially know existed.

PinkCurve should allow the discovery journey to evolve naturally.

---

# Daily Discovery Experience

PinkCurve is designed around continuous discovery.

Rather than expecting Buyers to search for everything they need, PinkCurve can present a living discovery experience that changes as worthwhile Offerings become available.

Examples may include:

- **For You**
- **New**
- **Trending**
- **Nearby**
- **Deals**
- **Verified**
- **Local Events**
- **Community**
- **Free**
- **Seasonal**
- **Limited-Time Offers**
- **Public Information**
- **Recommended for You**

The objective is not to fill the Buyer's screen continuously.

The objective is:

> **Show worthwhile things at worthwhile times.**

Over time, PinkCurve should become a place Buyers naturally consider opening because they expect there may be something worth discovering.

---

# Timely Discovery

Some Offerings become meaningful because of time.

Examples include:

- Sales events
- Seasonal promotions
- Holiday discounts
- Limited-time Offers
- Free products or services
- Product introductions
- Local events
- Community programs
- Public announcements
- Registration deadlines
- Temporary services
- Newly available resources

These are not necessarily news.

They are information that may be worthwhile for a Buyer to know **at the appropriate time**.

PinkCurve should therefore consider timing as an important discovery signal.

A worthwhile Offering presented after it expires has little discovery value.

A relevant Offering presented when the Buyer can act on it may have significant value.

> **Meaningful Discovery includes discovering the right thing at the right time.**

---

# First-Time Buyer Experience

A first-time visitor should understand PinkCurve primarily through the experience rather than through lengthy explanations.

Within the first few seconds, the visitor should understand something reasonably close to:

> **PinkCurve helps me discover things that may be worth my attention.**

The visitor should not initially be required to:

- Read a long explanation
- Understand PinkCurve's AI
- Understand Adaptive Metadata Navigation
- Create a complicated search query
- Configure extensive filters
- Understand Seller advertising
- Understand PinkCurve's business model

PinkCurve should demonstrate value before asking the visitor to understand the platform.

A possible first-time journey is:

```text
Open PinkCurve
      ↓
See Discovery Experience
      ↓
View Initial / Demonstration Offerings
      ↓
Swipe / Explore
      ↓
Understand PinkCurve's Value
      ↓
Register
      ↓
Create Initial Buyer Discovery Profile
      ↓
Begin Personalized Discovery
```

The exact registration point should be validated during Alpha.

PinkCurve may allow a limited discovery demonstration before registration so that a new visitor can understand the value of the platform.

Full ongoing Buyer participation may require registration.

---

# Buyer Registration

Buyer registration supports important PinkCurve capabilities, including:

- Discovery continuity
- Buyer Discovery Profile
- Personalized discovery
- Saved Offerings
- Buyer preferences
- Buyer feedback
- Brand Recognition relevance
- Trust
- Bot and abuse prevention
- Learning across sessions

Registration should not require Buyers to provide large amounts of information immediately.

PinkCurve should follow a progressive approach:

> **Ask for information when it creates understandable Buyer or platform value.**

The minimum registration requirements and progressive profiling model are defined through the Open Decision process and should be validated during Alpha.

See PD-005: Buyer Registration Model in [Open Decisions](19-open-decisions.md).

# Buyer Discovery Profile

A Buyer Discovery Profile helps PinkCurve understand what may matter to a Buyer.

It may progressively incorporate permitted information such as:

- Buyer-provided interests
- Explicit preferences
- Preferred categories
- Location preferences
- Price interests
- Discovery interactions
- Saved Offerings
- Not Interested signals
- Adaptive Metadata Navigation choices
- Buyer feedback
- Other consented discovery signals

The Buyer Discovery Profile should evolve rather than requiring the Buyer to define everything during registration.

PinkCurve should not assume that a historical profile always represents current intent.

A Buyer must be able to redirect discovery.

The profile assists discovery.

It should not imprison the Buyer inside past behavior.

---

# Buyer Intent

Current Buyer intent can be more important than historical behavior.

For example, a Buyer who normally explores electronics may currently be looking for:

- A restaurant
- A local event
- A home service
- A gift
- A community program

PinkCurve should therefore combine appropriate discovery signals rather than relying on a single source.

Conceptually:

```text
Buyer-Provided Information
        +
Buyer Discovery Profile
        +
Current Buyer Intent
        +
AMN Interactions
        +
Permitted Discovery History
        +
Context / Location
        +
Offering Knowledge
        +
Trust Signals
        ↓
AI-Assisted Discovery
        ↓
Meaningful Discovery
```

AI should help interpret Buyer intent.

The Buyer should remain capable of changing direction at any time.

---

# The Offering as the Primary Experience

Each Offering should be treated as a visual discovery object rather than a traditional listing.

The graphic, image, short video, or visual story should occupy most of the available display area.

Supporting information should appear only when it adds immediate discovery value.

A conceptual mobile experience might resemble:

```text
┌─────────────────────────────┐
│ For You   Nearby   Deals    │
│                             │
│                             │
│                             │
│       VISUAL OFFERING       │
│       IMAGE / VIDEO         │
│                             │
│                             │
│                             │
│  Nearby   Deal   Verified   │
│                             │
│ ♡  Not Interested  Explore →│
└─────────────────────────────┘

             ↑
           Swipe
```

This is conceptual rather than a fixed UI specification.

Actual mobile and desktop designs should be prototyped and tested with Buyers.

The central principle remains:

> **The Offering receives the Buyer's attention. The interface should not compete with it.**

---

# Discovery Signals

Discovery Signals are concise Buyer-facing interpretations of PinkCurve's metadata, context, trust information, or discovery intelligence.

Examples may include:

`Nearby` · `Deal` · `New` · `Verified` · `Free` · `Trending` · `Ending Soon`

Other signals may indicate why an Offering may matter:

- Matches Your Interests
- Near You
- New Today
- Popular Nearby
- Limited Time
- Community Event
- Verified Seller

Only signals that materially help discovery should appear.

PinkCurve should not decorate Offerings with large numbers of labels simply because metadata exists.

---

# Rich Metadata Underneath, Minimal Metadata on Screen

Rich metadata is essential to PinkCurve.

Metadata may include:

- Offering type
- Category
- Price
- Location
- Seller information
- Trust information
- Availability
- Timing
- Discounts
- Audience relevance
- Campaign information
- Features
- Benefits
- Discovery characteristics
- Other Offering Knowledge

However, Buyers should not be required to see everything PinkCurve knows.

PinkCurve follows the principle:

> **Rich metadata underneath. Minimal metadata on screen.**

Metadata should be progressively disclosed.

---

## Level 1 — Immediately Visible

Only a few high-value Discovery Signals should appear.

For example:

`Nearby` · `Deal` · `New` · `Verified`

These indicators should remain compact and should not substantially obstruct the Offering's visual presentation.

---

## Level 2 — Available on Demand

When the Buyer becomes interested, PinkCurve may reveal additional information such as:

- Price
- Distance
- Seller
- Location
- Category
- Offer details
- Availability
- Expiration
- Trust information
- Why this may be relevant
- Other useful Offering information

---

## Level 3 — PinkCurve Intelligence

Detailed metadata used by PinkCurve's:

- Retrieval
- Ranking
- AI
- Personalization
- Adaptive Metadata Navigation
- Trust
- Fraud detection
- Learning
- Analytics

should primarily remain behind the Buyer Experience.

The Buyer benefits from the intelligence without having to manage its complexity.

---

# Adaptive Metadata Navigation

Metadata is not merely descriptive information.

Metadata can become a navigation system.

PinkCurve calls this approach:

> **Adaptive Metadata Navigation (AMN)**

AMN allows Buyers to progressively direct discovery through meaningful metadata without repeatedly leaving the visual experience to configure complex filters or search forms.

For example:

- Tap **Nearby** → discover nearby Offerings
- Tap **Deals** → discover current promotions
- Tap **New** → discover recently available Offerings
- Tap **Free** → discover free products, services, or resources
- Tap a **Category** → continue within that category
- Tap a price characteristic → adjust price direction
- Tap a location characteristic → change geographic discovery
- Tap a trust indicator → understand its meaning
- Tap **Not Interested** → reduce similar results

AMN should adapt to:

- The Offering being viewed
- Available Offering Knowledge
- Buyer intent
- Buyer Discovery Profile
- Current navigation context
- Relevant discovery choices

The objective is not to expose every possible metadata attribute.

The objective is to expose the **most useful next navigation choices**.

---

# Buyer Control

AI recommendations should not take control of discovery away from the Buyer.

PinkCurve should always provide understandable mechanisms that allow Buyers to redirect the experience.

Examples include:

- Show something different
- Change category
- Show nearby
- Show deals
- Show new Offerings
- Show free Offerings
- Change price direction
- Not Interested
- Explore a different interest
- Start a new discovery direction

PinkCurve should learn from Buyers without making them feel trapped by what the platform previously learned.

> **AI assists discovery. The Buyer directs the journey.**

---

# Fast Visual Navigation

Buyers should be able to move rapidly through Offerings.

Primary interactions may include:

- **Swipe** — Move to another Offering
- **Tap metadata** — Continue discovery through AMN
- **Explore** — Learn more from the Seller
- **Save** — Return to an Offering later
- **Not Interested** — Reduce similar Offerings and provide a discovery signal
- **Trust Information** — Understand relevant trust signals
- **Share** — Share an Offering where appropriate
- **Redirect Discovery** — Change the direction of discovery

Buyers should not repeatedly have to return to menus.

Navigation should feel continuous.

---

# Negative Feedback and "Not Interested"

Positive interaction alone does not tell PinkCurve what Buyers want.

Negative feedback is also valuable.

A Buyer should have an easy way to indicate:

> **Not Interested**

This action should:

- Remove or move past the Offering
- Provide a Discovery Event
- Help PinkCurve understand the Buyer's current intent
- Improve future retrieval or ranking where appropriate
- Help AMN adapt
- Help the Learning Engine improve

PinkCurve should be careful not to overinterpret one negative action.

A Buyer may reject an Offering for many temporary reasons.

Negative feedback should therefore be treated as a signal rather than a permanent definition of the Buyer.

---

# Progressive Disclosure

PinkCurve should reveal information according to Buyer interest.

The conceptual progression is:

```text
SEE
 ↓
Is this potentially interesting?
 ↓
INTERESTED
 ↓
Show useful details
 ↓
EXPLORE
 ↓
Visit Seller destination
```

This protects the visual experience while making deeper information available when it becomes useful.

---

# Trust Is Part of Discovery

Trust is not a separate destination inside PinkCurve.

It is part of the discovery experience.

PinkCurve should work to reduce Buyer exposure to:

- Fake Sellers
- Fake Offerings
- Scams
- Misleading Offerings
- Suspicious destinations
- Fraudulent behavior
- Bots and abuse
- Other potentially harmful activity

Trust information should be communicated visually and clearly wherever practical.

PinkCurve should never imply that an Offering is completely risk-free.

Instead, PinkCurve should provide understandable evidence, verification information, warnings, and protections that help Buyers make better-informed decisions.

---

# Trust Signals Without False Guarantees

Potential trust indicators may include:

- Seller verification
- Offering verification
- Verified contact information
- Location information
- Seller history
- Destination verification
- Suspicious-activity warnings
- Other PinkCurve trust signals

Every trust indicator must have a clearly defined meaning.

For example:

> **Verified**

must identify what PinkCurve actually verified.

It must not imply that PinkCurve guarantees:

- The Seller
- The Offering
- The transaction
- Product quality
- Seller performance
- Buyer satisfaction
- A future outcome

unless PinkCurve explicitly provides such a guarantee.

---

# Approval Is Not Permanent

Seller and Offering conditions can change.

A Seller may change:

- Ownership
- Website
- Contact information
- Business practices
- Offering information
- Destination pages

An Offering may change or expire.

A previously legitimate destination may become compromised.

Therefore:

> **PinkCurve approval should not be considered permanent.**

Trust and approval systems should support:

- Periodic reevaluation
- Risk-triggered reevaluation
- Seller changes
- Offering changes
- Destination changes
- Buyer reports
- Automated risk signals
- Human review where appropriate

Buyer-facing trust signals should reflect PinkCurve's current available evidence rather than historical approval alone.

---

# Seller Destination Experience

PinkCurve facilitates discovery.

The Seller generally owns the transaction and customer relationship.

When the Buyer selects **Explore**, PinkCurve should provide a clear transition to the appropriate Seller destination.

Conceptually:

```text
PinkCurve Discovery
       ↓
Buyer Finds Worthwhile Offering
       ↓
Explore
       ↓
Approved Seller Destination
       ↓
Buyer Investigates Further
       ↓
Seller Handles Transaction
```

The transition itself is part of PinkCurve's Buyer Experience.

PinkCurve should therefore evaluate Seller destinations during approval and periodically thereafter where appropriate.

Potential checks include:

- Destination exists
- Destination corresponds to the Offering
- HTTPS/security basics
- No obvious malicious behavior
- Seller identity consistency
- Offering consistency
- Reasonable mobile usability
- No obvious deceptive behavior
- Destination remains active

AI may assist these evaluations.

Human review should remain available for cases requiring judgment.

PinkCurve cannot guarantee the quality of every Seller website.

However, PinkCurve should not knowingly direct Buyers to clearly suspicious, misleading, broken, or inappropriate destinations.

---

# Mobile and Desktop Experience

PinkCurve should be designed mobile-first.

However, Meaningful Discovery should also work effectively on:

- Desktop browsers
- Tablets
- Other appropriate future interfaces

The experience does not have to be identical across devices.

The principles should remain consistent:

- Visual First
- Simple navigation
- Minimal clutter
- Fast discovery
- Buyer control
- Progressive disclosure
- Trust visibility
- AMN
- Easy Seller exploration

Responsive design should be used where appropriate.

Mobile and desktop prototypes should be tested independently because screen size and interaction behavior differ.

---

# Discovery Intelligence Behind the Experience

The simplicity of PinkCurve's interface should not limit the intelligence underneath it.

PinkCurve may use:

- Offering Knowledge
- Buyer Discovery Profiles
- Buyer intent
- Adaptive Metadata Navigation
- Discovery Events
- Embeddings
- Vector retrieval
- Ranking
- AI models
- Location relevance
- Context
- Timing
- Trust Signals
- Learning models
- Seller information

to determine which Offerings may be worthwhile.

The Buyer does not need to know:

- Which retrieval system found the Offering
- Which model ranked it
- Which AI model analyzed it
- Which embeddings were used
- Which metadata attributes were considered
- Which Learning Engine process influenced the result

The Buyer needs to experience the outcome:

> **Something potentially worthwhile appeared quickly and was easy to understand.**

# Discovery Events and Learning

Buyer interactions should generate appropriate Discovery Events that allow PinkCurve to understand and improve the discovery experience.

A Discovery Event should contain sufficient identifiers and context to make the event useful and auditable.

Depending on the event type, information may include:

- Event ID
- Timestamp
- Buyer ID
- Offering ID
- Seller ID where appropriate
- Session ID
- Event type
- Discovery context
- AMN interaction
- Discovery path
- Device context
- Relevant location context where permitted
- Ranking/model context where required
- Other necessary analytical information

Discovery Events may include:

- Offering shown
- Offering viewed
- Swipe
- Metadata selection
- AMN selection
- Save
- Not Interested
- Share
- Explore
- QOV
- Trust interaction
- Buyer feedback

Discovery Events should support:

```text
Buyer Experience
       ↓
Discovery Events
       ↓
Discovery Analytics
       ↓
Learning Engine
       ↓
Improved Discovery
       ↓
Better Buyer Experience
```

Privacy, consent, retention, and applicable U.S. legal requirements must govern the collection and use of Buyer information.

# Buyer Experience Success Test

PinkCurve should periodically test the experience with people who have never used the platform.

A simple test remains valuable:

1. Give the person PinkCurve without explaining it.
2. Allow the person to use it.
3. Ask:

> **"What do you think PinkCurve helps you do?"**

A successful experience should produce answers reasonably close to:

> "It helps me discover products, services, deals, events, or other things that might interest me."

An even stronger result would be:

> "It shows me things worth discovering."

If Buyers instead:

- Do not know what to do
- Cannot identify PinkCurve's purpose
- Feel overwhelmed
- Need extensive instructions
- Cannot redirect discovery
- Cannot understand why information is being shown
- Cannot distinguish PinkCurve from an advertising feed

the Buyer Experience requires improvement.

PinkCurve should explain itself primarily through use.

---

# Meaningful Discovery Test

Usability alone is not enough.

A Buyer may understand how PinkCurve works while still receiving little value.

PinkCurve should therefore test:

> **Did the Buyer actually discover something worthwhile?**

Important questions include:

- Did the Buyer find something useful?
- How quickly?
- How much irrelevant content was encountered first?
- Was navigation understandable?
- Did AMN help?
- Did the Buyer feel overwhelmed?
- Did the Buyer feel in control?
- Did the Buyer trust the experience?
- Did the Buyer want to continue discovering?
- Did the Buyer want to return?
- Did the Buyer Explore an Offering?
- Did the Buyer discover something they did not previously know existed?

The objective is not maximum interaction.

The objective is Meaningful Discovery.

# Product Design Principles

## Meaningful Discovery First

Showing more is not necessarily better.

Help the Buyer discover something worthwhile.

---

## Visual First

Buyers primarily discover through graphics, images, videos, and visual stories rather than text-heavy listings.

---

## Buyer Control

AI assists discovery.

The Buyer directs the journey.

---

## Metadata Without Clutter

PinkCurve may maintain rich Offering Knowledge and metadata while displaying only what the Buyer needs at that moment.

---

## Adaptive Navigation

Metadata should help Buyers navigate discovery rather than simply describe Offerings.

---

## Discovery Before Complexity

The Buyer should not need to understand PinkCurve's AI, ranking systems, agents, advertising infrastructure, Seller tools, or Learning Engine.

---

## Progressive Disclosure

Information should appear when Buyer interest makes it useful.

---

## Timely Discovery

An Offering's value may depend on when the Buyer discovers it.

---

## Trust by Design

Trust and Buyer protection are integrated into discovery rather than added afterward.

---

## Learn Without Trapping the Buyer

PinkCurve should learn from Buyer interactions while allowing the Buyer to change direction at any time.

---

## Sophisticated Underneath. Simple on the Surface.

PinkCurve's intelligence belongs behind the experience.

The Buyer should simply feel:

> **PinkCurve helps me discover worthwhile things.**

---

# Core Buyer Promise

PinkCurve helps Buyers discover worthwhile products, services, promotions, events, community resources, public information, and other Offerings through a simple, visual, intelligent, and trustworthy discovery experience.

PinkCurve does not seek to overwhelm Buyers with everything available.

PinkCurve seeks to help each Buyer discover **what matters**.
