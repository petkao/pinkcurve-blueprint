## Overview

PinkCurve pricing should reflect the value created through Meaningful Discovery.

PinkCurve is not primarily selling impressions.

PinkCurve is not primarily selling advertising space.

PinkCurve is not processing transactions and taking a percentage of Seller sales.

PinkCurve helps Sellers place worthwhile Offerings in front of relevant Buyers and helps Buyers discover Offerings that matter.

The pricing strategy should therefore begin with a simple principle:

> **PinkCurve should charge Sellers for measurable discovery value—not simply for exposure.**

Pricing must also support the long-term sustainability of PinkCurve.

PinkCurve incurs real costs for:

- AI
- Infrastructure
- Offering ingestion
- Offering Knowledge
- Seller verification
- Offering approval
- Trust and fraud prevention
- Discovery
- Analytics
- Learning
- Customer Support
- Billing
- Engineering
- Operations
- Security
- Legal and compliance
- Marketing
- Continuous platform improvement

PinkCurve cannot create a trusted, high-quality Discovery Platform through permanently unsustainable pricing.

At the same time, Sellers must believe that the value they receive justifies what they pay.

The long-term objective is therefore:

> **Fair Seller pricing + measurable discovery value + sustainable PinkCurve economics.**

# Pricing Philosophy

## Charge for Discovery Value

PinkCurve should seek to charge for identifiable value delivered to Sellers.

The primary example is the Qualified Offering Visit (QOV).

A QOV represents a qualified Buyer visit from PinkCurve to the Seller's destination after discovery has occurred.

This is more meaningful than merely displaying an Offering.

However, not every PinkCurve Seller service creates value in the same way.

Different services may require different pricing models.

---

## Pricing Must Preserve Buyer Relevance

Seller spending must not override Buyer relevance.

A Seller who spends more should not automatically receive higher discovery ranking for an Offering that is less relevant to the Buyer.

Seller spending may legitimately determine:

- Campaign budget
- Geographic campaign scope
- Brand Discovery scope
- Campaign duration
- Creative Studio usage
- Analytics capabilities
- Seller Intelligence services
- Other clearly defined Seller services

But Seller spending should not purchase the right to degrade Meaningful Discovery.

> **Money may fund discovery. It should not determine relevance.**

This principle must remain true even if violating it could produce more short-term revenue.

---

## Pricing Must Be Understandable

A Seller should understand:

- What PinkCurve charges for
- When a charge occurs
- How the charge is calculated
- What activity produced the charge
- How much has already been spent
- How much budget remains
- What services are included
- What services cost extra
- What credits or adjustments were applied

Pricing should not require Sellers to become advertising-pricing experts.

> **A Seller should understand the cost before spending the money.**

---

## Sellers Must Control Their Spending

PinkCurve should provide Sellers with appropriate controls over their spending.

Depending on the service, controls may include:

- Monthly budget
- Campaign budget
- Daily budget
- QOV limit
- Maximum QOV price where appropriate
- Geographic scope
- Category scope
- Campaign start date
- Campaign end date
- Brand Discovery budget
- Creative Studio usage limits
- Notifications
- Spending alerts
- Automatic campaign pause
- Other appropriate limits

A Seller should not receive a large unexpected invoice because PinkCurve generated activity beyond a budget the Seller intended to spend.

> **Seller budget control is part of Seller trust.**

---

## Pricing Consistency and Negotiation

PinkCurve should seek to provide fair, understandable, and consistent
pricing rather than requiring Sellers to negotiate for better prices.

For standard PinkCurve services, published pricing should generally apply
equally to Sellers operating under comparable conditions.

A Seller should not need superior negotiating power, company size,
personal relationships, or purchasing expertise to receive fair pricing.

> **PinkCurve should seek to make its pricing fair before the Seller
> needs to negotiate it.**

### Standard Pricing

Standard Seller services should generally use defined pricing rules.

Pricing may legitimately vary based on factors such as:

- Offering category
- Geography
- Service level
- QOV volume
- Campaign scope
- Brand Discovery scope
- Creative Studio usage
- Seller Intelligence capabilities
- Other documented pricing factors

These differences should result from defined pricing policies rather
than arbitrary individual negotiation.

### Seller Pricing Questions

Sellers should always be able to question pricing.

PinkCurve should explain:

- How the price was determined
- What the Seller receives
- How the price compares with applicable pricing tiers
- What lower-cost options may be available
- How the Seller can control spending

Listening to a Seller's pricing concern is different from negotiating
an arbitrary discount.

Seller feedback about pricing should be captured and used in PinkCurve's
pricing studies.

### Enterprise and Exceptional Arrangements

Some future enterprise or unusual arrangements may require customized
pricing because the service itself is customized.

Examples may include:

- Very large discovery volumes
- Specialized integrations
- Dedicated services
- Custom analytics
- Large geographic programs
- Unique operational requirements

In these situations, PinkCurve may establish custom pricing based on the
actual scope and economics of the service.

Custom pricing should have documented rationale and appropriate approval.

It should not become arbitrary bargaining.

### Pricing Fairness

PinkCurve should periodically examine whether Sellers receiving
comparable services are being treated consistently.

> **Pricing flexibility may reflect differences in value or cost.
> It should not reflect differences in bargaining power alone.**

---

## PinkCurve Must Remain Economically Sustainable

Low pricing is not automatically good pricing.

PinkCurve should understand:

- Cost to serve
- Infrastructure cost
- AI cost
- Trust and verification cost
- Customer Support cost
- Seller acquisition cost
- Buyer acquisition cost
- Billing cost
- Fraud cost
- Operational cost
- Required margin
- Long-term investment requirements

Pricing should eventually support the continued operation and improvement of PinkCurve.

Unsustainable pricing may temporarily attract Sellers but eventually harms both Sellers and Buyers if PinkCurve cannot maintain the service.

---

# Do Not Underprice Meaningful Discovery

PinkCurve should not assume that being a new platform means it must permanently be the cheapest platform.

A lower unit price does not necessarily create better Seller value.

For example, a low-cost click from another platform may have little relevance to the Seller.

A higher-priced PinkCurve QOV may potentially represent substantially greater value if the Buyer reached the Seller through genuine Meaningful Discovery.

Therefore, PinkCurve should compare:

> **Cost relative to value delivered**

rather than simply:

> **PinkCurve price relative to competitor price.**

PinkCurve should seek competitive value, not necessarily the lowest price.

> **Meaningful Discovery should not be undervalued simply because PinkCurve is new.**

Early introductory pricing may be appropriate.

Permanent unsustainable underpricing is not.

# Qualified Offering Visit Pricing

## Qualified Offering Visit

A Qualified Offering Visit (QOV) is a Buyer visit from PinkCurve to the Seller's destination that satisfies PinkCurve's defined qualification requirements.

The exact QOV qualification rules must be validated and documented.

A QOV should represent more than an arbitrary click.

Potential qualification requirements may include:

- Valid human activity
- Valid PinkCurve session
- Legitimate Offering interaction
- Valid Seller destination
- Bot screening
- Fraud screening
- Duplicate handling
- Other qualification criteria defined by PinkCurve

The QOV definition should remain understandable to Sellers.

---

## Billable QOV

A QOV becomes billable only when it satisfies the applicable qualification and billing rules.

PinkCurve should maintain sufficient evidence to substantiate billable QOVs.

Known invalid activity should not intentionally become billable Seller activity.

---

## Invalid and Non-Billable Traffic

PinkCurve should identify and exclude appropriate activity such as:

- Known bots
- Detected fraudulent activity
- Invalid sessions
- Duplicate activity according to defined rules
- PinkCurve testing traffic
- Internal administrative traffic
- Known system errors
- Other activity that does not satisfy QOV qualification

Fraud detection will never be perfect.

However:

> **PinkCurve should not knowingly charge Sellers for activity it has determined to be invalid.**

---

# QOV Pricing Variables

QOV value may differ significantly.

Potential variables include:

- Offering category
- Seller industry
- Geography
- Buyer intent
- Offering value
- Market competition
- Seller economics
- Discovery quality
- Other validated factors

A QOV for a low-priced consumer product may not have the same economic value as a QOV for a high-value professional service.

Therefore, PinkCurve should not assume that one universal QOV price is appropriate.

The pricing model must be validated during Alpha and Beta.

# Market Pricing Research

PinkCurve should not establish pricing in isolation.

Seller alternatives already exist.

PinkCurve should understand what Sellers currently pay to obtain:

- Traffic
- Awareness
- Leads
- Discovery
- Customer acquisition
- Brand recognition
- Qualified interest

Competitive and adjacent platforms may include:

- Search advertising platforms
- Social advertising platforms
- Visual discovery platforms
- Marketplaces
- Local discovery services
- Industry-specific advertising channels
- Other emerging Discovery Platforms

The purpose of competitive pricing research is **not to copy competitor pricing**.

The purpose is to understand the market in which Sellers make spending decisions.

> **Competitive pricing is an input to PinkCurve pricing, not the determinant of PinkCurve pricing.**

---

## Pricing Research Framework

For each relevant platform, PinkCurve should periodically evaluate:

| Dimension | Research Question |
|-----------|------------------|
| Pricing Model | CPC, CPM, CPA, subscription, auction, fixed, other? |
| Seller Objective | Awareness, discovery, traffic, leads, sales? |
| Billable Event | What causes the Seller to be charged? |
| Typical Cost | What market range can be observed? |
| Budget Control | What spending controls are available? |
| Targeting | Geography, category, interests, intent, audience? |
| Relevance | How does relevance affect delivery or price? |
| Measurement | What performance evidence does the Seller receive? |
| Fraud Protection | How is invalid traffic treated? |
| Minimum Spend | What minimum budgets or bids exist? |
| Seller Accessibility | Can small Sellers participate economically? |
| Market Position | Mass-market, premium, enterprise, self-service? |
| Seller Support | What support is provided when billing is questioned? |

---

## Compare Equivalent Value

PinkCurve should avoid simplistic comparisons such as:

> Competitor click = $X  
> PinkCurve QOV = $Y

The units may not represent equivalent value.

Instead, PinkCurve should investigate:

```text
Seller Spending
      ↓
Traffic / Discovery Delivered
      ↓
Quality of Buyer Interest
      ↓
Seller Destination Visits
      ↓
Seller-Observed Outcomes
      ↓
Value Created
```

The relevant benchmark is:

> **What does the Seller currently spend to obtain comparable discovery value?**

This may be more useful than simply comparing CPC numbers.

# Brand Discovery Pricing

Brand Discovery creates a different form of Seller value from a QOV.

A Seller may want relevant Buyers to become familiar with:

- A brand
- A new product family
- A new service
- A Seller location
- A seasonal campaign
- A business entering a new market

The objective may be recognition rather than immediate Seller-site visits.

Therefore, Brand Discovery should not automatically use the QOV pricing model.

- Category

- Frequency

The Seller pays for the defined Brand Discovery service.

But PinkCurve must still protect Buyer relevance.

> **Paying for Brand Discovery does not purchase the right to become irrelevant discovery.**

PinkCurve should provide strong Seller budget controls.

Potential controls include:

- Monthly maximum
- Campaign maximum
- Daily limit
- QOV maximum
- Brand Discovery maximum
- Creative Studio allowance
- Start and end dates
- Automatic pause
- Spending notifications

Possible notifications may include:

90% of budget used

> **A Seller should never need to fear an uncontrolled PinkCurve bill.**

# Billing Must Be Supported by Evidence

PinkCurve should be able to substantiate the charges presented to Sellers.

A Seller should not receive an invoice containing only an amount due.

The Seller should be able to understand:

- What PinkCurve delivered
- What activity was billable
- How the charge was calculated
- When the activity occurred
- Which Offerings or campaigns generated the activity
- What rate was applied
- Which activity was excluded
- What credits were applied
- How invalid, duplicate, fraudulent, or bot traffic was handled

The objective is not merely billing accuracy.

The objective is Seller confidence.

> **Every material Seller charge should be supported by understandable evidence.**

---

# Phone-Bill Principle

PinkCurve billing should follow a principle similar to a detailed telephone or utility bill.

The Seller first receives an understandable summary.

Supporting detail is available when the Seller wants to investigate the charges.

For example:

```text
PinkCurve Monthly Statement
Billing Period: August 1–31

Offering: Example Offering
-----------------------------------------
Qualified Offering Visits          126
Rate per QOV                     $X.XX
QOV Charges                    $XXX.XX

Brand Discovery Campaign          $XX.XX

Creative Studio                    $0.00
Included with plan

Credits / Adjustments             -$X.XX
-----------------------------------------
TOTAL                           $XXX.XX
```

The exact format will evolve.

The principle should remain:

> **Summarize clearly. Preserve sufficient underlying evidence to substantiate the bill.**

---

# QOV Billing Evidence

PinkCurve should maintain sufficient underlying records to substantiate billable QOV activity.

Depending on privacy requirements and the final QOV definition, supporting information may include:

- QOV ID
- Timestamp
- Offering ID
- Seller ID
- Campaign ID where appropriate
- Permitted session reference
- Discovery context
- Qualification status
- QOV pricing classification
- Applied rate
- Fraud/bot validation status
- Duplicate handling
- Billing status
- Adjustment status

Buyer privacy must remain protected.

> **Sellers need evidence of billable discovery activity—not unnecessary access to Buyer identity.**

# Billing Transparency During the Billing Period

Sellers should not have to wait for an invoice to understand what they are spending.

Where practical, Seller dashboards should display:

- Current spending
- Budget remaining
- QOV count
- Estimated current charges
- Campaign spending
- Brand Discovery spending
- Credits
- Adjustments
- Invalid activity excluded
- Billing-period dates

> **The invoice should confirm what the Seller has already been able to observe—not surprise the Seller.**

# Billing Questions and Customer Support

Billing questions should be treated as an important Seller Customer Support responsibility.

When a Seller questions a charge, PinkCurve should be able to trace:

```text
Seller Question
      ↓
Invoice
      ↓
Invoice Line Item
      ↓
Billable Activity
      ↓
QOV / Campaign / Service
      ↓
Discovery Events
      ↓
Qualification / Fraud Checks
      ↓
Pricing Rule
      ↓
Charge Explanation
```

Customer Support should have tools to perform routine billing investigations without requiring engineering assistance.

AI may assist Customer Support by:

- Retrieving billing records
- Explaining calculations
- Summarizing QOV activity
- Identifying possible duplicates
- Identifying credits
- Detecting unusual patterns
- Preparing evidence
- Escalating unusual cases

Human review should remain available for:

- Disputes
- Ambiguous cases
- Significant adjustments
- Suspected fraud
- System errors
- Other consequential cases

---

# Billing Corrections

PinkCurve should acknowledge and correct legitimate billing errors.

When PinkCurve determines that a Seller was incorrectly charged, the platform should have a documented process for:

- Correcting the charge
- Issuing appropriate credit or refund
- Explaining the correction
- Recording the reason
- Investigating the cause
- Preventing recurrence where possible

PinkCurve should not make legitimate billing disputes unnecessarily difficult simply to protect short-term revenue.

> **Correcting a billing error can create more long-term value than defending an incorrect charge.**

---

# Billing Trust

Billing is part of PinkCurve's Seller relationship.

A Seller who believes PinkCurve:

- Charges fairly
- Provides evidence
- Answers questions
- Corrects mistakes
- Protects budgets
- Excludes known invalid traffic
- Delivers meaningful value

is more likely to continue participating.

This can lead to:

```text
Billing Confidence
      ↓
Seller Trust
      ↓
Seller Retention
      ↓
More Offerings
      ↓
Greater Seller Participation
      ↓
Better Buyer Discovery
      ↓
PinkCurve Reputation
      ↓
Sustainable Growth
```

> **Seller trust is earned not only by delivering value, but by proving the value for which PinkCurve charges.**

# Cold-Start Pricing Strategy

PinkCurve faces a fundamental early-stage challenge:

```text
Buyers need worthwhile Offerings
        ↑
        │
        │
        ↓
Sellers need participating Buyers
```

Neither side becomes sufficiently valuable without the other.

Therefore, cold-start pricing should be treated differently from mature-platform pricing.

The purpose of cold-start incentives is:

> **Create sufficient Seller and Buyer participation for Meaningful Discovery to begin working.**

---

# Founding Seller Program

Early Sellers take additional risk by participating before PinkCurve has demonstrated a large Buyer audience.

PinkCurve should recognize this contribution.

Possible Founding Seller benefits may include:

- Free Alpha participation
- QOV credits
- Introductory QOV pricing
- Free or assisted Offering ingestion
- Creative Studio allowances
- Brand Discovery trial credits
- Early Seller Intelligence access
- Beta pricing incentives
- Direct feedback opportunities
- Other clearly defined temporary benefits

Founding Seller programs should be designed carefully.

PinkCurve should generally avoid promises such as:

- Lifetime free service
- Permanent extreme discounts
- Unlimited free usage
- Other obligations that could damage future sustainability

A better principle is:

> **Early participation may receive clearly defined temporary benefits in exchange for helping PinkCurve validate and improve the platform.**

---

# Founding Buyers

Cold start is not only a Seller problem.

PinkCurve must also attract enough Buyers to create meaningful discovery activity.

Founding Buyer acquisition may require:

- Marketing
- Demonstration experiences
- Community outreach
- Referral programs
- Local launch strategies
- Category-focused launches
- Promotional discovery
- Other Buyer acquisition efforts

Buyers should remain free to use PinkCurve.

However, Buyer acquisition still creates real PinkCurve costs.

# Cold-Start Participation Before Revenue Optimization

During early Alpha, the primary objective should not be maximizing Seller revenue.

The priority should be validating:

- Seller participation
- Buyer participation
- Offering quality
- Meaningful Discovery
- QOV definition
- Seller value
- Buyer value
- Trust
- Pricing willingness
- Billing accuracy
- Support processes

Charging too aggressively before PinkCurve has proven value could prevent the ecosystem from forming.

Charging too little for too long could create unrealistic expectations and unsustainable economics.

Therefore:

> **Cold-start pricing should optimize participation and learning without destroying the path to sustainable pricing.**

# Alpha Pricing Strategy

Alpha should primarily validate the PinkCurve model.

Possible Alpha approach:

- Buyers: Free
- Sellers: Free or heavily credited
- Offering ingestion: Free within defined limits
- QOV billing: Simulated or credited
- Brand Discovery: Experimental
- Creative Studio: Limited allowance
- Seller Intelligence: Basic included
- Billing: Fully tested even if payment is not collected

This last point is important.

Even if Sellers are not actually paying during Alpha, PinkCurve should test:

```text
Discovery Event
      ↓
QOV
      ↓
Pricing Rule
      ↓
Invoice
      ↓
Seller Statement
      ↓
Billing Question
      ↓
Customer Support Resolution
```

Alpha can therefore test the billing system without creating unnecessary financial friction.

### Pricing Discovery During Alpha

PinkCurve should expect additional pricing questions to emerge during
Alpha that cannot reasonably be anticipated before real Sellers and
Buyers use the platform.

These questions should be treated as valuable evidence rather than
unexpected problems.

Material pricing questions should be documented, evaluated through the
Pricing Study, and, where appropriate, added to Chapter 19 — Open
Decisions with required evidence, decision ownership, decision timing,
and validation requirements.

> **Alpha should discover the right pricing questions as well as help
> determine the right prices.**

# What PinkCurve Will Not Do

PinkCurve should establish clear pricing boundaries.

PinkCurve will not intentionally:

- Sell relevance to the highest bidder
- Charge Sellers for known bot traffic
- Charge Sellers for activity PinkCurve has determined to be fraudulent
- Hide material pricing terms
- Make ordinary billing evidence unnecessarily difficult to obtain
- Prevent legitimate billing questions
- Make legitimate billing corrections unnecessarily difficult
- Intentionally allow spending beyond defined Seller limits without authorization
- Sacrifice Meaningful Discovery simply to increase short-term billing
- Exploit Buyer attention as an unlimited resource
- Permanently underprice the platform in ways that make quality service unsustainable

Pricing should reinforce PinkCurve's philosophy rather than undermine it.

- Founding Seller incentives
- Alpha billing approach
- Beta pricing
- Community/public-service pricing
- Credit and refund rules

These decisions should be tracked through Chapter 19 — Open Decisions.

Each significant pricing decision should include:

- Status
- Why it matters
- Options
- Work required
- Evidence required
- Decision deadline
- Decision owner
- Implementation impact
- Final decision
- Decision rationale
- Validation result

# Long-Term Pricing Evolution

PinkCurve pricing will evolve as the platform learns.

Early pricing may emphasize participation.

Later pricing may increasingly reflect demonstrated discovery value.

The evolution may look conceptually like:

```text
Alpha
Participation + Learning
        ↓
Beta
Pricing Validation
        ↓
Initial Production
Evidence-Based Pricing
        ↓
Growth
Category / Geographic Refinement
        ↓
Maturity
Value-Based Discovery Economics
```

The pricing mechanism may change.

The underlying principle should remain:

> **Create measurable value for Sellers, protect Meaningful Discovery for Buyers, and maintain a sustainable PinkCurve platform.**

---

# Pricing Strategy North Star

Every significant pricing decision should ultimately answer four questions:

1. **Does the Seller understand what they are paying for?**
2. **Can PinkCurve substantiate the value being billed?**
3. **Does the pricing preserve Meaningful Discovery for the Buyer?**
4. **Can PinkCurve sustainably provide and improve the service at this price?**

If the answer to any of these questions is no, the pricing model requires further examination.
